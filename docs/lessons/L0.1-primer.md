# Domain primer: breast pathology and whole-slide images

*Lesson L0.1 deliverable. Copy to `docs/domain/primer.md` and make it your own.*

## 1. Why this primer exists

This platform detects and grades breast cancer in digital pathology slides. Every engineer on the project (that's me, for now) needs a shared vocabulary for the tissue, the stains and the image files, because those words become entity fields, API parameters and model inputs.

## 2. From tissue to digital slide

```
Biopsy or resection → formalin fixation → paraffin block (FFPE)
→ 3–5 µm section → glass slide → stain (H&E or IHC) → coverslip
→ whole-slide scanner → WSI file (.svs, .tif, .ndpi, .mrxs, DICOM)
```

Each step can introduce artifacts (folds, blur, bubbles, pen marks, uneven staining), which the platform's quality control must detect.

## 3. Whole-slide images in one page

- A WSI is a **gigapixel** image: e.g. 80,000 × 60,000 px at 0.25 µm/px ≈ 4.8 gigapixels ≈ 14 GB uncompressed.
- It is stored as compressed **tiles** in a **pyramid** of resolution levels. Level 0 is full resolution; each higher level is downsampled (Aperio: 1, 4, 16, …).
- **mpp** (microns per pixel) converts pixels to physical size; ~0.25 at "40×", ~0.5 at "20×".
- Platform rule: all coordinates are **level-0 pixel coordinates**, and mpp is stored per slide.
- Files may contain **label** and **macro** images that can identify a patient; the platform removes them on ingestion.

## 5. Breast cancer: what the platform detects and grades

- **Detection**: where is the tumor? Tumor vs normal per patch, region and slide. First data: PatchCamelyon and CAMELYON16 (lymph node metastases of breast cancer).
- **Grading**: how aggressive does it look? Nottingham grade from tubule formation, nuclear pleomorphism and mitotic count (each 1–3; total 3–5 = grade 1, 6–7 = grade 2, 8–9 = grade 3). Mitotic counts are per area, so grading depends on correct mpp.

### Tissue and lab

1. **Histopathology**: diagnosing disease by examining thin slices of tissue under a microscope.
2. **Biopsy / resection**: a biopsy removes a small tissue sample (often by needle); a resection removes a larger piece in surgery (e.g. lumpectomy), often with lymph nodes.
3. **FFPE (formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded)**: the standard way tissue is preserved and hardened in wax so it can be cut into thin sections.
4. **Frozen section**: tissue frozen and cut quickly during surgery; faster than FFPE but lower image quality. TCGA marks these `TS`/`BS`, diagnostic FFPE slides `DX`.
5. **Section**: a slice of tissue about 3–5 µm thick, cut with a microtome and mounted on a glass slide.

### Stains

6. **H&E (hematoxylin and eosin)**: the routine stain. Hematoxylin colors nuclei blue-purple; eosin colors cytoplasm and stroma pink.
7. **IHC (immunohistochemistry)**: a stain using antibodies to show whether a specific protein is present; positive areas turn brown (DAB) on a blue counterstain.
8. **ER / PR / HER2 / Ki-67**: the four key breast cancer IHC markers: estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, HER2 growth receptor, and the proliferation marker Ki-67.
9. **Stain variation**: differences in color between labs, batches and scanners for the same stain; a major cause of AI models failing on new data.

### Breast cancer

10. **DCIS (ductal carcinoma in situ)**: abnormal cells confined inside the breast ducts, not yet invasive.
11. **Invasive carcinoma (NST / IDC, ILC)**: cancer that has spread beyond the ducts or lobules into surrounding tissue. NST (formerly IDC) is the most common type; ILC (lobular) the second.
12. **Lymph node metastasis**: cancer cells that have spread to a lymph node; detecting them is the CAMELYON16 task.
13. **Nottingham grade**: the breast cancer grading system combining tubule formation, nuclear pleomorphism and mitotic count into grade 1, 2 or 3.
14. **Mitosis**: a cell in the process of dividing; mitotic count per area is one of the three grading features.
15. **Nuclear pleomorphism**: how much nuclei vary in size and shape; more variation means a higher score.

### Whole-slide images

16. **WSI (whole-slide image)**: a digital, microscope-resolution image of an entire glass slide.
17. **Whole-slide scanner**: a device that photographs a slide tile by tile through a microscope objective and stitches the result.
18. **Gigapixel**: a billion pixels; typical WSIs are 1–10 gigapixels, too large to load into memory at once.
19. **Tile**: a small square region (e.g. 256×256 px) stored and decoded independently, so software reads only what it needs.
20. **Patch**: a tile used as input to a machine learning model (e.g. 96×96 px in PatchCamelyon).
21. **Pyramid / resolution levels**: the same image stored at several resolutions, from level 0 (full) to small overviews, for fast zooming.
22. **Downsample factor**: how much smaller a pyramid level is than level 0 (e.g. 4 means one pixel covers 4×4 level-0 pixels).
23. **Level-0 coordinates**: pixel coordinates at full resolution; the platform's single coordinate system.
24. **mpp (microns per pixel)**: physical width of one level-0 pixel in micrometres; converts pixels to real size.
25. **Objective magnification (20×, 40×)**: the microscope lens label; approximately 0.5 and 0.25 mpp respectively, but mpp is the precise value.
26. **Associated images (label, macro, thumbnail)**: extra small images inside a WSI file; the label and macro can contain patient identifiers.
27. **Vendor formats (SVS, NDPI, MRXS, TIFF, DICOM WSI)**: file formats from different scanner makers; DICOM WSI is the hospital standard.
28. **OpenSlide**: an open-source library that reads most WSI formats through one API.

### Image quality and analysis

29. **Tissue mask**: a binary image marking which areas contain tissue versus empty glass background.
30. **Artifacts**: unwanted features from preparation or scanning: folds, blur, air bubbles, pen marks, dust.
31. **Annotation / ROI (region of interest)**: a shape drawn on a slide (e.g. tumor outline), stored in the platform as GeoJSON.
32. **Brightfield**: imaging with light passing through the slide (H&E and IHC); QuPath asks for this image type.
